# Hanceville Wants a New Police Name After Corruption Scandal Wrecked the Old One

Hanceville, Alabama dissolved its police department after a corruption investigation wrecked criminal cases, exposed major evidence-room failures, and led to indictments across the department.

Now the city is preparing to rebuild the force under a different brand.

Mayor Nolan Bradford told WAFF in July that the city would probably change the department's name in part so that when people search for it later, they will not immediately see all of the negative material tied to the old Hanceville Police Department.

That negative material is not just a public-relations problem.

A Cullman County grand jury recommended abolishing the department and described a rampant culture of corruption. Five current or former officers and one officer's spouse were indicted.

An evidence-room audit later found more than seven ounces of methamphetamine and other drugs missing. At least one firearm appeared to be missing, while roughly 30 additional guns were present without proper documentation.

The damage reached actual prosecutions.

Cullman County District Attorney Champ Crocker said the grand jury dismissed **58 felony cases** because cases tied to the former department had become unprosecutable after officers' illegal actions. Most involved drugs.

The criminal cases against former department personnel are also producing guilty pleas. As of Aug. 19, WAFF reported guilty pleas from former Chief Jason Marlin, former officer Jason Wilbanks, Eric Kelso, and Donna Kelso. Two other defendants had not been reported as having resolved their cases.

So the sequence is unusually clear:

The evidence system failed.  
Dozens of felony cases collapsed.  
Officers and the chief were criminally charged.  
The department was dissolved.  
Guilty pleas followed.

And while rebuilding it, city leadership is considering changing the department's name partly because the old one produces ugly Google results.

Changing the sign is considerably easier than changing the record.
